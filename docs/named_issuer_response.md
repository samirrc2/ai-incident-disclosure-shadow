# Prepared response — naming of specific issuers (editorial/reviewer query)

*Standby note for editorial correspondence. Not intended to appear verbatim in the
manuscript; the manuscript's Ethics Statement and framing already implement its logic.*

A reviewer or editor may ask why specific issuers (e.g., Zillow, General Motors, Meta,
Tesla) are named. Our position:

1. **The classifications rest entirely on public records.** Every coding decision is
   derived from public SEC filings (EDGAR) and public AI Incident Database records. No
   private, confidential, or non-public information is used or disclosed.

2. **The paper measures disclosure incidence, not legal compliance.** We report whether an
   incident was disclosed in a company's securities filings within a defined window. We
   make **no** allegation, and draw **no** inference, that any issuer violated a disclosure
   obligation. Materiality is a legal determination we explicitly do not adjudicate.

3. **Naming is necessary for transparent, reproducible coding.** The study's core method is
   an incident-to-filing match. That match cannot be verified, reproduced, or audited
   without identifying the issuer and the specific filing. Anonymizing the issuers would
   make the central claim unfalsifiable and defeat the reproducibility that is the paper's
   principal strength.

4. **The same predefined rules were applied to every issuer.** All issuers were coded under
   one pre-registered codebook and decision tree, with an independent blind second-coding
   pass and a full-filing retrieval audit. No issuer was singled out; the named cases are
   simply the incidents that met the pre-specified T1/T2 criteria.

5. **Neutral language throughout.** The manuscript uses "no related disclosure located,"
   never "failed to disclose" or "concealed," and reports only aggregate rates in its
   headline findings. Individual issuers appear only where necessary to document the seven
   substantive disclosures and to make the coding auditable.

This is the standard basis on which empirical disclosure research (e.g., the cyber-breach
disclosure literature) names issuers, and it is consistent with *Frontiers*' research-
integrity and ethics requirements.
