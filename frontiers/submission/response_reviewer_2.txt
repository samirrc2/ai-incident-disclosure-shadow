- Table 6 now reports the exact issuer-role-by-disclosure-tier counts and shows the two-by-two substantive-disclosure contrast separately.

- We also added a leave-one-positive-out analysis over 22 perturbations of the seven substantive cases. The three-level p-value ranges from 0.016 to 0.225, while the two-by-two contrast never rejects. The section is therefore presented primarily descriptively, with the inferential statistics retained as diagnostic sensitivity evidence. Supplementary Table S6 reports all perturbations.

- The severity section now opens by stating that it is exploratory because only seven incidents are substantively disclosed.

- The caveat is quantified: power is 23.3% for a threefold Severe-versus-Limited difference, and approximately a sevenfold difference would be required for 80% power at the observed Limited-group rate.

- The manuscript therefore concludes only that a severity gradient is not detectable in this sample, not that severity is unrelated to disclosure.

- Table 4 reports the exact severity-by-tier counts with Clopper–Pearson intervals.

Q4. Briefly mention how future studies could use this framework under newer regulations, such as the EU AI Act.

*Addressed in Section 6, p. 15, lines 683–690; Section 2 (Related work), p. 3, lines 131–144.*

- Section 6 now identifies regulatory change as a direct extension of the design.

- Article 73 of the EU AI Act is discussed as creating a mandatory serious-incident record for high-risk AI systems that could be compared with subsequent securities disclosure.

- The manuscript states the resulting test directly: whether mandatory incident reporting narrows the gap between externally recorded events and incidents identifiable in securities filings.

- It also notes that future U.S. AI-specific guidance, including an Item 1.05-style current-reporting approach analogous to the cybersecurity regime, would permit the same design.

- The present estimates are therefore positioned as a pre-regime baseline for those future comparisons.
