RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 1. References give section, then printed margin line numbers of the revised PDF.

Q1 Estimand. See 3.1.1, L178-196; Abstract L3-24; 3.2 L234-237; 6, L618-622; Figure 1; Table S4. L179-182 state the estimand as Pr(substantive disclosure in 8-K/10-K/10-Q within 12 months | AIID-recorded, organization identifiable, issuer resolvable), your (a). L193-196 state the rates do not generalize and the bias is not identified. L183-192 quantify the three layers: 398 generic/individual actors, 243 private firms, 400 unresolved, 26 foreign private issuers, 15 delisted. The title now reads "Tracking AI Incidents into U.S. Securities Filings".

Q2 T3. See 3.4, L279-295; 4.2, L410-422; 3.7 L355-358; Tables 1, 3, 8, S2. T3 is now a closed rule: a passage must name a technology/application family or a harm family from Table 1. Abstract risk language, unrelated AI mentions, compensation exhibits, subsidiary lists and promotional language are excluded. We adopted your split: of 278 T3, 84 are T3a and 194 T3b, so only 30.2% of the generic mass matches both dimensions. T3b+T4 is 216/307, 70.4% (64.9-75.4). L419-420 state the split leaves 97.7% unchanged, since both stay outside T1 and T2. Labelled post-hoc; worked examples in Table S2.

Q3 Terminology. See 3.4, L296-300, L305-311; 4.1 L393-398; 5, L531-537; 7, L711-714; Tables 2, 3. The definition is now strictly descriptive, not identifiable in the specified filing universe under the protocol, and L308-311 add that it implies nothing about materiality, obligation, withholding or compliance. L531-537 list benign explanations: immaterial, no financial or legal consequence, indirect involvement, already public elsewhere. Table 2 reports the four tiers uncollapsed, Table 3 the five-way split.

Q4 Entity resolution. See 3.2, L203-237; 3.2.1, L238-245; 4.8 L507-511; 6, L628-634; Tables 5, 8, S4, S8, S9. A blind second coder re-derived the issuer for 80 incidents, oversampling all 27 multi-candidate cases, 30 subsidiary mappings and all 13 small-issuer cases: agreement 82.5%, kappa 0.79, AC1 0.82, 14 disagreements, none T1 or T2, adjudicated 10:4. L203-210 give the one-issuer rule and tie-break order, L211-215 the 47 subsidiary mappings and as-of-date checks with one failure, L216-221 the 137-row crosswalk review. Table 5 separates attribution basis from mention.

Q5 Severity. See 4.3, L423-437; 6, L659-663; Tables 4, S1, S5. The section is now exploratory, Table 4 gives exact counts with Clopper-Pearson intervals, and power is 23.3% for a threefold difference, sevenfold needed for 80%. L434-437 conclude only that no gradient is detectable. The 40 CSET-classified incidents contain one substantive case, so no test is run on that subset.

Q6 Role. See 4.4, L438-455; 5 L566-571; Tables 6, S6. Table 6 gives exact counts and a separate two-by-two panel. Leave-one-positive-out over 22 perturbations moves p from 0.016 to 0.225, reaching 0.05 in five, and the two-by-two never rejects. We therefore present it descriptively: all T1 cases are deployer-only or dual-role, and none of 58 developer-only incidents receives T1 or T2.

Q7 Filing universe. See 3.3, L247-250 and L257-262; Abstract L23; 6, L623-627; 7 L711-714. Investor disclosure is replaced by the named forms throughout, and L257-262 list the excluded channels: earnings calls, presentations, non-SEC releases and dockets, regulator announcements, websites, blogs and conferences. The outcome is visibility in a defined SEC filing universe, not all investor communication, and the title carries the same change.

Q8 Cybersecurity. See 2, L120-144; 6, L683-690. L131-144 separate template from analogy: cyber has Item 1.05 and Regulation S-K Item 106 and clearer event boundaries, while no U.S. AI duty applied, AI harms are diffuse without affected-person counts, and responsibility spans developer, deployer and platform. These differences require the attribution procedures of 3.2. L683-690 extend the design to Article 73 of the EU AI Act.
